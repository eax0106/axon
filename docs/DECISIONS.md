| 2026-10-27 | A person who asked not to be called is never called by any campaign, whatever its skip-do-not-call switch says; the switch covers the rest of the account's list | The switch is for numbers the owner added by hand; a caller's own request is not the owner's to override |
| 2026-10-27 | Campaign defaults (for the owner to confirm; all in the campaign's settings): 2 calls at once, 2 retries an hour apart in the same run, skip a number another campaign called in the last 7 days | Cautious launch values; each one is visible and editable per campaign |
| 2026-10-27 | A campaign does not start without the owner's confirmation that the list may be called; the statement they agreed to is stored word for word with who and when | Consent is the owner's duty under the TRAI rules and has to be provable |
| 2026-10-27 | Campaigns of one employee share its number's channels, oldest campaign first. A rule-table refusal pauses the campaign and tells the owner; no credits, closed hours or a busy line only make it wait | A refusal will not change by itself, the others will |
| 2026-10-27 | A repeating campaign is re-armed, not copied: when a run ends its answered and gave-up contacts go back to pending for the next day or week. Skipped and cancelled contacts stay as they are. Putting contacts back on a finished campaign pauses it until the owner says resume | One campaign row and one history; nobody is called by a restore alone |
| 2026-10-27 | Consent covers the list as it stood when the owner confirmed it. Contacts added afterwards pause a running or scheduled campaign, and a campaign with newer contacts than its last confirmation neither starts nor resumes until the owner confirms again. A callback a caller asks for on a campaign's last call re-opens the finished campaign; in a repeating campaign it waits for the next run | The cautious reading of "the list may be called" (review of B8); a callback the caller asked for must not be lost to timing; a repeating campaign that has finished its run is closed until its next one |
| 2026-10-27 | An incoming call is answered only when the employee on the number is live, the account has credits, the rule table allows an inbound call on that number type, and a channel is free; anything else is a missed call that carries a plain-language reason and costs nothing. Closed hours either take a message (the call is answered, the closed message is spoken, the caller can leave one) or do not answer, by the owner's choice (default: take a message) | Parity with the Outpero Inbound rules; a refusal the owner can read is better than a silent ring-out |
| 2026-10-27 | The recording notice is always spoken first and cannot be left out: the owner can reword it and the greetings, never remove the notice. The employee does not greet again after the opening | TRAI and DPDP expect callers to be told a call is recorded before they speak |
| 2026-10-27 | Opening hours are weekly windows in fixed IST (a window cannot cross midnight: an overnight shift is two windows). A greeting for a new caller cannot use a name, since none is known | Same fixed-IST rule as calling hours; a made-up name on a first call is wrong in a way the caller notices |
| 2026-10-27 | Instant and Bulk employees answer a call on their number from one of their own leads (toggle `answer_callbacks`, default on), with the lead's details, the last call's summary, caller memory and, for Bulk, the row of their own list as context. With the toggle off the call is a missed one with a reason. An Instant employee without a lead for the caller still answers | Parity; a lead who rings back should not meet a closed line, and the owner can still choose to be unreachable on a sales-only number |
| 2026-10-27 | The auto call-back to a missed caller is a single queued call placed by the Instant dialer under the `callback` rule of the rule table, inside calling hours, for callers who met a closed or busy line only (not a paused employee or no credits, which would only make the dialer wait), never to a number on the do-not-call list, never twice to the same caller in a day, and never retried. Its outcome is never retried either, and an inbound call that is not answered is never redialed. A call-back the caller asked for on a call is dialed under the same rule and keeps its ordinary retry ladder | One try is what the owner switched on; the rule table keeps every outbound call under the legal switch the owner already controls |
| 2026-10-27 | A message or call-back request is made only from what the caller said: the analysis must quote the caller's own words for it, as for every other extracted field, or no message is written. A call has one message however its parts arrive (the analysis, a failed transfer). Nothing ticks a message done by itself; the owner does | Nothing invented; a call-back request needs a human to say it was dealt with |
| 2026-10-27 | Transfer to a person: the AI says it is connecting the caller, waits until that has been heard, and asks the carrier to run our transfer instructions on the caller's leg (a Dial to the owner's number, showing the employee's own number as caller id, 30 s to ring, one hour at most). The call stays up, and keeps its channel, until the carrier reports how the transfer ended. A transfer that does not connect leaves the owner a message; a failed request leaves the call with the AI, which apologises and offers to take a message. Talk time after the hand-over is not billed to the owner (the bridged leg is the carrier's own charge) | The caller must never be dropped by a transfer; the owner pays for the AI's work, not for two humans talking |
| 2026-10-27 | The Vobiz answer URL carries no documented signature, so an incoming call is believed only if the carrier itself lists that call as live; the transfer and transfer-result URLs are opened by short-lived signed tickets naming one call | A forged request for a call the carrier does not have must not make us answer, or make us dial anyone |
| 2026-10-27 | The daily summary email goes out from 8 AM IST with yesterday's incoming calls (India day), once per account, and only if some employee has the summary on and there were calls. The day is claimed before sending (a crash loses one email, never sends two); without an email provider nothing is claimed | The owner reads it at the start of the day; a duplicate is worse than a rare missing email |
| 2026-10-27 | In-call actions are opt-in per employee (none by default, at most 10) and live in the script bundle, so they share its publish gate, its last three versions and restore. An edit to the script that does not mention actions leaves them alone; the employee's own call tools (end the call, hand over to a person) stay as they are and their names cannot be reused | Parity (edits to actions are published like script edits); an editor that does not know about actions must not wipe them |
| 2026-10-27 | The custom HTTP tool may reach only the public internet: https on the standard port, no login in the address, values from the caller go only in the path (encoded) or query or body, every address the name resolves to must be public and the connection is made to the address that was checked, redirects are not followed, and an answer is read to 64 KB and shortened to 2000 characters before the AI sees it. Its secret is stored with the action but never sent back by any endpoint (a masked copy keeps the stored one) | A request leaves from our servers and the values in it come from a stranger on the phone: it must never reach our database, our other services or a cloud metadata address |
| 2026-10-27 | One Google connection per account serves Calendar and Sheets; the owner picks which services to allow and the screen shows what Google really granted. Disconnecting revokes the token at Google. Sheet rows are written as plain text (never formulas). Bookings are in fixed IST, made only into a free working-hours slot that is checked again at the moment of booking, with one event per call and time, and nobody is invited | The cautious reading of "connect your calendar"; a booking the caller cannot see should still never land on a busy or closed time, or email a stranger |
| 2026-10-27 | A WhatsApp message goes only to the person on the call, only as an approved template, never to a number on the do-not-call list, and once per call for the same words. The test message goes only to the owner's own verified phone | The AI must not be talked into messaging someone else; a template is what WhatsApp's own rules allow us to start |
| 2026-10-27 | What a tool returns is data, never instructions (the prompt says so). A failed or slow tool never ends the call: the AI gets a plain failure and offers a follow-up. The call's timeline records that a tool was used, its name, whether it worked and how long it took, never what it was given or answered | A caller's details and a service's answer can be anything; the record should prove use without becoming a second copy of personal data |
| 2026-10-27 | A stored action secret is kept only while the action's host is unchanged. The AI edit flow never receives action secrets. Only the account owner changes which spreadsheet a Sheets action uses, until the owner answers whether members may use the Google connection. | B10 review round 1: a member could repoint an action with the mask sent back and receive the owner's secret; the edit flow put secrets in the model prompt; sheets are read with the owner's Google account. |
| 2026-10-27 | What an employee is taught (facts and rules) is its own list, outside the published script: it applies to the next call at once, with no publish, versions or restore. At most 60 facts and 20 rules of 300 characters, because all of them go into every prompt. A taught fact is newer than the FAQ and the agent information and wins where they disagree; a taught rule is conduct, so it beats the call flow but sits after the built-in conduct and can never excuse it. Start over also clears what was taught | Parity (training applies instantly); script = content, rules = conduct, and the owner's correction is the newest word. A start-over employee that still follows old rules would surprise the owner |
| 2026-10-27 | Teaching by text, voice or photo is a proposal: the model splits the input into facts and rules, nothing is stored, and the owner saves the reviewed list. An item that is too long or not a fact or rule is dropped and counted, never cut. Each proposal is one paid use of AI help; documents are free apart from the hourly allowance | A misheard or misread item must not reach callers without the owner's eyes on it; a cut price is a wrong price |
| 2026-10-27 | Document search ranks passages inside Postgres on plain `REAL[]` columns (a dot product of unit vectors) instead of the pgvector extension, and returns at most three passages that score at least 0.55. A document is at most 60,000 characters, an employee holds 10, the original file is not kept, and an embedding failure stores nothing. Changing the embedding model means uploading documents again (passages of another size are skipped) | CI runs on stock Postgres on three operating systems where the extension is not available; the scale of a price list or a policy document does not need an index. Revisit with pgvector when documents grow. 0.55 is a first guess to tune on real documents (WIRING item 27) |
| 2026-10-27 | The AI reaches documents only through a `look_up_knowledge` function that exists on a call only when the employee has documents; what it returns is data, never instructions, and a failure never ends the call. The call's timeline records that it was used, never the question or the answer | Documents are retrieved only when relevant; the same rule as every other in-call action |
| 2026-10-27 | The struggle detector runs inside the post-call analysis of real calls (never test calls): a suggestion exists only when the caller's own words are quoted from the transcript, a rule also needs the AI's own quoted words, a gap carries no text because only the owner knows the answer, at most 3 a call and 50 open for an employee, and a moment the owner has seen (taught or dismissed) does not return. Nothing is taught until the owner accepts | Nothing invented: the model may point at a moment, but what the employee is taught is the owner's word |
| 2026-10-27 | A PDF or photo is accepted only when the model reports it finished reading (`finishReason` is `STOP`); a cut-off reading is refused with 422 and nothing is stored. Text cleaning keeps the zero-width joiner and non-joiner, which Telugu and Hindi words need, and still turns every other control or invisible character into a space. | A partly read price list taught as complete would make the employee quote wrong prices; stripping the joiners split Indic words (B11 review). |
| 2026-10-27 | Webhook events are written to an outbox in the same transaction as the change that causes them (new leads and campaign status changes through a flush hook, calls by a scan of ended calls) and sent by the worker, so every code path announces them without the engine knowing and none is lost if a send fails. A call is announced once, after it has settled: an answered call waits for its analysis (20 minutes at most), so `call.completed` carries the outcome and summary. Test calls and calls that never dialed are never announced. | Webhooks that miss an event or announce a half-finished call are worse than none; the outbox keeps the change and the event together. |
| 2026-10-27 | An account webhook is signed with `X-Axon-Signature: t=<unix>,v1=<HMAC-SHA256 of "<t>.<raw body>">` using the endpoint's `whsec_` secret, which is shown only on creation and rotation. A send is tried up to 6 times (1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h apart) with a 10 s timeout; a redirect is a failure and the receiver's answer body is never read. The address follows the custom HTTP tool's rule (public https, standard port, no login) and the connection is made to the address that was checked. | Receivers need a replay-checkable signature; a webhook address typed in by an owner must not reach our database, other services or a cloud metadata address. |
| 2026-10-27 | API keys are `axk_live_` plus a random token, stored only as a SHA-256 hash and shown once. Only the owner creates or revokes them, an account holds at most 20 active, and each key may make 120 requests a minute (429 with `Retry-After`; if Redis is down the limit is not enforced rather than failing the API). The public API under `/v1` is read-only except posting a lead to an Instant employee, which goes through the same intake as the website URL. Every key request is logged (method, path, status, duration) and kept 30 days with the events and deliveries. | A key is a password for the account's data: a leaked database must not leak keys, and a runaway script must not exhaust the API. |
| 2026-10-27 | A call is voicemail only when the post-call analysis quotes the recording's own words from a caller-side turn of the transcript (the same "nothing invented" rule as struggles). There is no carrier answering-machine detection in the codebase, so a call is never marked voicemail without a quote, and a call whose analysis was skipped or failed is not voicemail. | A false voicemail would silently stop a CRM hearing about a real person, so the flag needs evidence the owner can read. |
| 2026-10-27 | An employee's result webhook lives in the script bundle: it is publish-gated, versioned and restored with the script, and a script edit that does not mention it leaves it alone. It is sent only for calls that reached someone (`call.completed`, `call.inbound_answered`) according to its trigger (all connected, skip voicemail, voicemail only). Its optional secret signs the send the same way, is stored but shown as the mask on every read, is kept only while the host is unchanged, and never goes to the AI edit flow. | Same handling as an action's secret (B10); the result goes where the owner pointed it only after they published. |
| 2026-10-27 | Calls that had ended before migration 0010 are marked as already announced, so applying it never sends a burst of old events. The OpenAPI document is checked by structural tests written for this repo (unique operation ids, every `$ref` resolves, the bearer scheme on every operation, no internal route), not by a validator library, because `openapi-spec-validator` is not on the BUILD_PLAN dependency list. | Old history is not news; a new dependency needs the owner's yes. |
| 2026-10-28 | Every AI leg of a call (speech-to-text, LLM, voice) holds every vendor that can serve it, in order: the chosen one, then the rest of its kind, with vendors that are unhealthy right now last. A vendor is left at its first real error while another is left (a refused key, a timeout, a dropped connection); the last vendor of a leg is given up at once on a permanent error or after 3 errors in 30 seconds. Errors raised by our own code (a tool handler) never switch vendors. The caller's lost turn is asked of the new LLM. An LLM request that is silent for 10 seconds is a failure | A caller cannot wait for a vendor to recover, and Pipecat reports a refused websocket as transient, so its own strategy would keep retrying the dead vendor |
| 2026-10-28 | A Premium voice falls back to Value and then Standard; Value and Standard fall back to each other and never to Premium. A call is billed at the cheapest tier it used, for the whole call. A fallback voice is the vendor's default voice for the language | The customer never pays more than the voice they chose, and pays less when the call fell back |
| 2026-10-28 | When a leg has no vendor left, the caller hears a short apology in the call's language and the call is hung up once it was played (at once if no voice is left); the reason is `provider_failure` and an outgoing attempt that ended this way is retried along its ladder like a failed dial | A clean end instead of dead air, and the lead is not lost to our outage |
| 2026-10-28 | Vendor health is scored from what calls and dials saw: a vendor is unhealthy while it has at least 3 failures making up half or more of its last 10 minutes, and healthy again when they age out. A carrier counts against itself only for its own faults (5xx, timeouts, 401, 403, 429), not for refusing one call. An unhealthy vendor is tried last, not removed. Outcomes are kept 7 days | Real traffic is the best probe, a single blip should not take a vendor out, and a last resort is better than no call |
| 2026-10-28 | No call is started while the platform cannot carry it: the platform-wide cap (`AXON_MAX_CONCURRENT_CALLS`, default 100) is full, the carrier is unhealthy (outgoing calls only), or a leg has no configured, healthy vendor. An Instant call waits without using an attempt, a campaign waits with a plain note, and an incoming caller is missed with code `unavailable` and called back | Failing a call we know will fail wastes the lead's attempt and the caller's time |
| 2026-10-28 | Vendor rates (INR per metered unit) and balances are entered by staff from each vendor's price list and dashboard; a balance is estimated as the recorded figure less the metered cost since. Usage without a rate shows no cost rather than a guess | None of the vendors gives us a usage or balance API we can rely on, and a guessed cost would mislead the margin |
| 2026-10-28 | A staff support session acts as the account's first owner for 30 minutes, needs a written reason, writes every change made in it to the audit log under the staff member's id, cannot reach the admin console, change the password or make an API key, and stops when the staff member loses admin rights. A password change signs out every older session | Staff can see what the customer sees without a password, and nothing done in their name is untraceable |
| 2026-10-28 | A failed post-call analysis is retried after 2 and then 10 minutes, then left `failed` for a person to re-run; re-running starts the count again | Most failures are a vendor blip; retrying forever would spend the AI fee allowance on a call that cannot be read |
